The checkout price is only one part of a smart doorbell decision. The fairest comparison uses the same capabilities over the same length of time.

You do not need a spreadsheet. You need one formula and honest inputs.

## The formula

> True cost = hardware + accessories + installation + (monthly recurring cost × 12 × years)

For multiple devices, multiply the per-device hardware price by quantity before adding shared equipment. Be careful with subscriptions: some plans cover one device, while others cover an account or location. Use the current plan rule for the product and region you are pricing.

The [CompatNavi calculator](/cost/) applies this formula locally in your browser.

## 1. Hardware

Use the price you can actually buy at, including the correct model and required bundle. Do not compare one product's temporary sale price with another product's list price unless that is genuinely what you can pay today.

For a multi-door setup, enter the full quantity. Then check whether extra chimes or hubs are shared or required per door.

## 2. Accessories and storage

Common additions include:

- an indoor chime;
- a transformer or approved power adapter;
- a mounting wedge or corner kit;
- a second battery;
- a microSD card;
- a local hub or network video recorder;
- a PoE injector or switch;
- Ethernet cable and termination hardware.

Only add what your chosen installation needs. A product with built-in storage should not be charged for a card it will not use; a cloud-only product should not receive an imaginary local-storage advantage.

## 3. Installation

Battery mounting may be a straightforward do-it-yourself job, while transformer replacement or Ethernet routing can require paid work. Use a real quote when possible.

Installation is an upfront cost, but it can change the long-term outcome. Spending more to run Ethernet may make sense for a larger PoE camera system; doing it for one doorbell in a finished rental may not.

## 4. Subscription

Use the monthly equivalent of the plan that provides the feature you expect. If an annual plan is discounted, divide its annual price by 12. If a free trial ends, do not average the promotional period forever—price the paid period that follows.

Most importantly, compare equivalent recording capability. Ring states that reviewing previously recorded video requires a Ring Home subscription. Google lists some included history or previews on current Nest models, with longer retention and certain advanced features on Google Home Premium. Local-recording products may offer core history without a monthly fee but require storage hardware upfront.

Plan names, prices and features change. Check the manufacturer plan page on the day you compare.

## 5. Other recurring costs

This field is optional. Use it for professional monitoring, extra cloud storage, dedicated connectivity or another charge directly caused by the setup. Do not allocate the entire household internet bill to one doorbell unless that connection exists only for the device.

## Compare the output correctly

Look at three numbers:

- **Upfront total:** the cash needed to get the system working.
- **Recurring total:** subscriptions and other monthly charges over the chosen period.
- **Effective monthly cost:** the complete total divided by all months, useful for comparing different cost shapes.

For example, a local-storage setup may have the higher upfront figure but the lower three-year total. A cloud-first product may be cheaper to install but continue charging for history. Neither is automatically better—the calculation exposes the trade.

## Run two scenarios

Open the [true cost calculator](/cost/) twice or note each result:

1. price the exact features you need over three years;
2. repeat over five years;
3. use the same currency and capability level;
4. separately judge non-price differences such as outage behavior and ecosystem support.

Cost should narrow a compatible shortlist, not rescue an incompatible product. Use the [fit checker](/check/) first if power, network or storage is still uncertain.
