Power choice changes more than installation. It can affect charging, continuous recording, chime behavior, network reliability and the accessories you must buy.

Begin with the building: what is already at the door, what can be added safely, and who is allowed to do the work?

## Battery: simplest placement, recurring maintenance

A battery model is the natural starting point when there is no usable doorbell wiring or cable route. Installation can be less invasive, and several current models also accept wiring later.

Check whether the battery is removable or built in. A removable pack can be swapped while the doorbell remains mounted; a built-in battery may require taking the unit down to charge. Battery-life estimates depend on activity, settings, temperature, signal and viewing habits, so treat them as test-condition estimates rather than a schedule.

Also ask what “wired mode” does on a battery product. The [Ring Battery Doorbell 2K Plus specifications](https://ring.com/support/products/doorbells/battery-doorbell-2k-plus?page=1) say compatible wiring charges its quick-release battery. The [eufy E340 page](https://www.eufy.com/products/t8214111) says the battery must remain installed in wired mode.

Battery is a strong fit when easy placement matters more than avoiding charging, and when event-based recording is enough.

## Traditional doorbell wiring: verify the transformer

A dedicated wired model can remain powered and may support features that are difficult on battery, including continuous recording on eligible products and plans.

But “existing wires” are not the full requirement. You need the correct voltage, transformer capacity and chime arrangement. Google specifies 16–24 VAC and 10–40 VA for the Nest Doorbell wired 3rd generation. Ring lists 10–24 VAC with a 40 VA maximum for its Video Doorbell Wired support page in the cited US-oriented documentation. Requirements differ, so inspect the transformer label and exact installation manual.

Some battery-or-wired models unlock continuous recording only with particular wired power. The [eufy C31 product FAQ](https://www.eufy.com/products/t8223111) says its 24/7 mode requires a 16–24 V, 30 VA transformer and bypassing the existing chime. The [Tapo D225 page](https://www.tapo.com/us/product/smart-doorbell/tapo-d225/) also limits 24/7 recording to hardwired mode.

If you are not qualified to inspect or change low-voltage wiring, include professional installation in the budget.

## PoE: one data cable, deliberate installation

Power over Ethernet can carry network data and electrical power through one Ethernet cable. It avoids dependence on Wi-Fi at the door and can suit a local NVR setup.

It is not plug-and-play unless the infrastructure exists. You need a cable route and a compatible PoE switch, injector or NVR. Reolink lists IEEE 802.3af, 48 V active PoE for its Video Doorbell PoE, along with Ethernet, microSD and Reolink NVR recording.

PoE is attractive during construction, renovation or an existing camera-network installation. It can be excessive when running cable would require major work for one doorbell.

## Quick decision table

| Your situation | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| No wires and drilling must be minimal | Battery |
| Compatible transformer and chime already exist | Wired or battery-plus-wiring |
| Continuous local recording is important | Wired or PoE products that explicitly support it |
| Wi-Fi at the door is unreliable and Ethernet is possible | PoE |
| You rent or expect to move the device | Battery, subject to mounting permission |
| You already operate an NVR and PoE network | PoE |

## Do these checks before ordering

1. Photograph the existing chime, transformer label and wires.
2. Read the exact model's voltage and chime requirements.
3. Confirm whether wiring powers the device directly or only maintains a battery.
4. Confirm which recording modes each power option enables.
5. Price the transformer, chime, card, injector, switch, NVR and labor you actually need.
6. Run the candidate through the [fit checker](/check/).

The cleanest product page cannot compensate for the wrong infrastructure. Let the building narrow the shortlist first.
